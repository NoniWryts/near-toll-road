NEAR investment thesis

Does NEAR's fee switch pay for the chain?

NEAR Intents is a toll road for cross-chain swaps, and the NEAR token owns it. We follow the money from each swap to the token, using only public on-chain data.

Why this matters

Every blockchain has to pay the computers that run it. Most pay by printing new coins. On Ethereum, 93% of what stakers earn is newly minted ETH, and on Solana it is over 90% (Bitwise, Q2 2026). Nobody sends a bill. Holders just own a slightly smaller share of the network each month.

NEAR prints about 2.7 million new NEAR a month too. The difference is that since February, a fee on every swap through its NEAR Intents product is turned into NEAR and parked in a wallet. It is the first time a chain’s own product has paid toward that bill, and you can watch it happen on-chain.

So we asked how big that product has to get. Today it covers about 11% of what NEAR prints. It would need about 5 times the current traffic to cover all of it. That makes owning NEAR a bet on Intents growing, not on the fee switch as it stands today. If it does not grow, we wrote down in advance when we would walk away.

The answer
The mechanism is real, but small. For every 10 new NEAR printed to pay validators, the fee switch buys back about 1.
To pay for itself at today's 2.5% issuance, NEAR Intents would need about $630M of swaps a day, 5.0x what it does now. The issuance cut passed on 2 November (HSP-029) lowers that bar to $403M a day once fully phased in (about 2029).

Three things to know

It is real

100% of payouts traced
Every USDT payout from the bridge's Tron wallet in one week ($97.0M) matches a public NEAR withdrawal. The money is not invented.

It is small

10.9% of new supply
Buybacks absorb about a tenth of the NEAR printed. The rest is new supply, so each holder's share of NEAR still shrinks.

The bar is coming down

$630M → $403M a day
Holders voted to cut issuance (HSP-029). Once phased in, the volume needed to pay for the chain falls by a third.

Before the switch

Network pages →
Before the buybacks, usage was already shrinking, new NEAR was being printed at twice today's rate, and there was no revenue to buy with.
The fee switch went live on 2026-02-23; the first sweep reached the buyback wallet on 2026-04-23, 59 days later. This is a before-and-after description, not proof of cause: issuance was halved and a reward app stopped in the same months.
Monthly averages of active and new accounts per day. Active accounts fell from 883k a day in January 2025 to 233k a day in the months before the switch (-74%); transactions fell -47%.

1. There was no revenue to spend

Intents collected almost no fees until 2025-06-11, and NEAR's own cut was about $36 in total before November 2025 (DefiLlama). A buyback needs a stream of income to buy with; the stream only appeared in late 2025.

2. The first fix was a smaller printing press

A community proposal on 24 June 2025 argued that 5% a year of new NEAR was far more than the network needed. The halving (to 2.5%) took effect with the nearcore 2.9.0 protocol upgrade in late October 2025. Issuance in our data drops from about 4.8% to 2.4% a year in November.

3. Burning gas was too small to matter

NEAR burns part of every gas fee, but the proposal put the burn at about 0.1% of supply in a year. By our estimate (70% of gas fees) it covered only 2.8% of the value of new NEAR between November and the switch.

What could break it

All risks →
7 risks, ranked. Two are serious today; all are checked against the data on every rebuild.
We keep the bad news in. A thesis that hides its weak points can't be trusted on its strong ones.
High risk Bought-back NEAR is not retired
Buybacks page →

What we see

The 2.25M NEAR bought so far sits liquid in a multisig wallet (buybacks.multisignature.near). None of it is burned, staked or locked (0 NEAR locked; ledger C02 is FALSE).

Why it matters

A buyback only shrinks supply if the tokens stay out of circulation. Today the signers could sell or spend them, so our coverage figure is the best case, not a guarantee.

What would ease it

An on-chain burn, or a lock that only a holder vote can undo.
High risk Swap volume is falling
Fees page →

What we see

30-day volume is $3.8B, 21% below the 30 days before. DefiLlama's monthly change reads -19%.

Why it matters

Every dollar of buyback starts as a swap. Breakeven already needs 5.0x today's volume, and the gap widens when traffic drops.

What would ease it

Volume back above its September level, and a path toward $403M a day once HSP-029 is phased in.
Medium risk Buybacks are a choice, not a rule
Buybacks page →

What we see

Only 70% of NEAR's cut reached the buyback wallet in the last 30 days. The rest stayed in other revenue wallets.

Why it matters

No contract forces revenue into buybacks. The people running the wallets decide how much goes in and when, so the rate can drop without notice.

What would ease it

A published, on-chain sweep rule (for example: all revenue swept weekly).
Medium risk A few market makers fill most swaps
Volume quality page →

What we see

The largest solver fills 36% of sampled swap value; the top five fill 77%.

Why it matters

If one large market maker steps back, prices on Intents get worse, users leave and fees fall. The toll road depends on a handful of operators.

What would ease it

The top solver's share falling below a third as new solvers join.
Medium risk Fewer developers are building
Developers page →

What we see

580 monthly-active developers, down 26% in a year and 60% from the 2022 peak of 1,804. Full-time developers are up 4% to 268.

Why it matters

New apps bring new swap traffic. NEAR ranks 9 of 13 peer chains on active developers, so the pipeline of future fee payers is thin.

What would ease it

Two quarters of growth in monthly-active developers, not just full-time ones.
Medium risk Private volume cannot be checked
Volume quality page →

What we see

NEAR reports part of its volume as 'confidential'. Its share read 0% on 2026-11-11 and 0% on 2026-11-07, on the same total.

Why it matters

Nobody outside can see that private flow. Our custody check matched 100% of one week of USDT payouts on Tron to public withdrawals, but other routes are not reconciled.

What would ease it

NEAR publishing a verifiable total for confidential volume, or more routes passing our custody check.
Low risk The issuance cut could slip
Predictions page →

What we see

Holders passed HSP-029 on 2 November. It sits in a timelock until 16 November and then phases in over about two years.

Why it matters

Until it is live, breakeven stays at $630M a day. A delay or reversal keeps the bar at today's level.

What would ease it

The protocol's inflation setting changing on chain (our prediction P1 checks this).
